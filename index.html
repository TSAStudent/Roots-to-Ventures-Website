Why we exist

The gap is not drive. It is access.

Every first-gen immigrant student in DFW carries a story about a family that started over. A lot of them carry business ideas too. What they usually do not have is what turns an idea into a company.

01

Money stays home

When every dollar already has a job, a business camp is the first thing cut. 56% of immigrant teens name personal finances as a top barrier.

02

No one who looks like them

Most of these students have never met a founder with their background. When you cannot picture someone like you doing it, it is easy to decide it is not for you.

03

Programs built for someone else

Most programs assume students already have networks, free time and money to spare. They skip the cultural and money pressure first-gen students carry every day.

How it works

Four Saturdays. From an idea to a real pitch.

Three live Zoom workshops, each led by a mentor who has built something, then an in-person Pitch Day with seed prizes and a private meeting with a venture capitalist for the top three teams. Completely free.

Workshop 2

Pitch Development

Pitch Day

Live competition

Pitch in person at Nancy Watten Center High School. The top 3 teams win seed prizes and meet a VC.

Meet Venture.bot

An AI co-founder that keeps teaching between Saturdays.

After every workshop we feed the mentor’s frameworks into Venture.bot, so it gets smarter each week right along with the students. It stays free and open after the program ends.

Built for first-gen founders

Advice that understands the money and family context our students actually navigate, not generic startup tips.

Follows the workshop order

Problem, then plan, then pitch. Venture.bot reinforces what students learned that same Saturday.

VC prep

Practice investor questions before the real meeting, and keep building long after the last workshop.
